Calculation basis

Structural model

Each beam is analysed independently as a straight, prismatic, linearly elastic member with two vertical pin/roller reactions at the lifting lugs. Equal unloaded overhangs extend beyond both lugs. The full beam self-weight is included, including overhangs. Payload point loads and UDL patches must lie between the lugs. All actions are in the major-axis plane.

Load combinations

Strength: γG × beam self-weight + γQ × dynamic factor × assigned payload.
Service: beam self-weight + dynamic factor × assigned payload.

There is no automatic code-specific dynamic allowance, design category or lifting-device factor. Select and justify all factors for the intended use. Default factors 1.2G + 1.5Q reproduce the supplied strength model's combination, not a full AS 4991 design basis.

Analysis & deflection

Reactions follow force and moment equilibrium. Shear and moment are calculated with Macaulay functions. Maximum moment includes zero-shear locations and load boundaries; shear considers both sides of discontinuities. Deflection includes Euler–Bernoulli flexure and shear deformation using the entered effective shear area. Peak deflection is sampled at 501 locations. Displacements at both lugs are zero.

Capacity checks & load ceiling

Bending ratio = maximum absolute design moment / entered design bending capacity. Shear ratio = maximum absolute design shear / entered design shear capacity. Deflection ratio = maximum absolute service displacement / (lug spacing / selected divisor). A bisection search scales every payload together, preserving position, width and sharing. Self-weight stays fixed. The first ratio reaching 1.00 defines the displayed member-analysis ceiling.

The envelope repeats this calculation for each centred UDL width. It does not interpolate between point-load and UDL capacities. Bending–shear interaction is not calculated: where required, the entered capacities must account for the actual combined actions, or a separate interaction check is needed.

Default section data

460 UB 67.1; mass 67.1 kg/m; Iₓ = 295.8 × 10⁶ mm⁴; E = 200,000 MPa; G = 80,000 MPa; effective shear area = 3,859 mm². Per-beam fully restrained section benchmarks: 399.33 kNm bending and 625.16 kN shear, obtained from the supplied Tedds section calculations. Changing the section name or stiffness properties does not automatically change capacities; edit every applicable property and capacity.

Proof-test loading preset

Three overlapping UDLs: 196 kN over 5.0 m, 98 kN over 2.5 m, and 59 kN over 1.0 m, all centred. Total 353 kN shared equally across two beams. Factors are set to 1.0 to compare elastic response with the saved actual-loading model. This reproduces the model load pattern, not a verified test certificate.

What needs separate verification

  • Lateral torsional buckling, torsional restraint and the effectiveness of contact with the deck.
  • Axial compression from inclined slings, combined axial/bending/shear action, and eccentric lug forces.
  • Lug net section, bearing, tear-out, block shear, out-of-plane loading and pin/shackle compatibility.
  • Weld groups, local flange/web effects, load introduction and stiffeners.
  • Deck integrity, component connections, load sharing, centre of gravity and lift stability.
  • Material evidence, fabrication, inspection, fatigue, proof testing and approved WLL marking.

For inclined slings, T = vertical reaction / cos θ and H = vertical reaction × tan θ are force-demand estimates. No axial–bending capacity check is performed. A common angle with unequal end reactions does not establish horizontal equilibrium; the actual rigging geometry needs separate analysis.

Test discrepancy

The historic ratio 28.8 / 36 = 0.8 is not adopted as a strength multiplier. An unexplained test-deflection difference does not establish a proportional loss of strength. The flexural stiffness multiplier affects EI only and is provided for sensitivity analysis.
